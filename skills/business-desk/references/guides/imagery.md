# Business Desk social imagery guide

Use this with the [Social Posting Guide](social-posts.md). The writing guide develops the message. This guide decides whether a picture helps, what it should communicate, and how to make it readable on a phone.

The default is to give an image a clear job. Choose it when it explains something, shows relevant evidence or context, or adds a feeling that belongs with the story. A complete, useful text post can stand on its own.

This October 2026 guide combines selected public teaching, recorded visual observations, platform references, and Business Desk synthesis. The bundled briefs preserve coverage limits. Source images and bulk post archives are not distributed. These references do not represent the people.

## The 18 questions answered

### 1. When should a post have an image?

Ask what the reader gains by seeing it. Use a relevant photograph for a real moment, a screenshot for something actually demonstrated, or a diagram for a relationship that is easier to see. When the picture only adds decoration and the text already carries the whole point, start with text alone. This decision rule is Business Desk synthesis from Janis’s emphasis on communicating an idea and Jack’s teaching about making intangible things visible. It is not a proven platform ranking rule. [Janis on communicating ideas](https://ozo.art/archive/make-average-ideas-look-extraordinary/), [Jack on visual leverage](https://visualizevalue.com/blog/how-to-create-visual-leverage).

### 2. What job should the image do?

Write its job in one sentence: “Help the reader understand this comparison,” “Show what actually happened,” “Give the story a real setting,” or “Express the feeling behind this point.” Attention is useful when it leads to the intended meaning. Janis teaches planning the viewer’s reaction and exploring the idea before drawing. [Planning the reaction](https://ozo.art/archive/want-engagement-evoke-emotional-reaction/), [Writing before drawing](https://ozo.art/archive/overlooked-step-before-explaining-ideas-visually/).

### 3. Which types of posts benefit from which images?

These are starting choices to test with the owner’s audience.

| Post | Useful starting image | What it contributes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Personal reflection | A relevant original photograph, or text alone | A real moment and human context |
| Comparison or lesson | A simple diagram or illustration | A relationship the reader can see |
| Tutorial | A focused screenshot or short sequence | The actual step being explained |
| Verified result | A genuine photograph, permitted testimonial, or chart | Evidence with enough context to interpret it |
| Service explanation | A simple process diagram | How the service works |
| Announcement | A real subject photograph or restrained graphic | The specific person, event, or offer |

These mappings are Codex synthesis. The service diagram is an application of Jack’s visual leverage teaching. The comparison is an application of Janis’s comparison teaching. [Visual leverage](https://visualizevalue.com/blog/how-to-create-visual-leverage), [Meaningful comparisons](https://ozo.art/archive/how-to-make-your-visuals-pop-with-smarter-comparisons/).

### 4. How should the picture and text work together?

Let the picture show the setting, relationship, proof, or feeling. Let the caption explain its meaning and develop the useful point. Some overlap can make the pair clearer. Chris Do’s inspected portrait post repeats its central line on the picture and expands it in the caption. Visualize Value’s inspected geometric comparison leaves the concept name to the caption. Both are observed approaches, not a universal rule against repetition. [Chris’s portrait example](https://x.com/thechrisdo/status/1859413724421030373/photo/1), [Visualize Value’s comparison](https://x.com/visualizevalue/status/1370423341954306056/photo/1).

### 5. How do we choose the image format?

Use a photograph for an actual subject or moment. Use a screenshot when the interface or result is the subject, with unrelated private information removed. Use a diagram for steps, choices, or connections. Use a chart for verified quantities, with dates, units, and a source. Use an illustration for a conceptual metaphor. Use a text graphic for a short line worth emphasizing, with the source credited when it belongs to someone else.

This format selection is a Business Desk workflow. A rising line used as a metaphor must not imply measured results. The inspected Visualize Value discipline graphic illustrates that distinction. [Conceptual line example](https://x.com/visualizevalue/status/1366761195844337665/photo/1).

### 6. When is an AI image appropriate?

Use AI for an original conceptual illustration, a metaphor, or a designed scene when that serves the idea. Use an actual asset when the post describes the owner, a customer, a completed job, a real product interface, or a measured outcome. An invented picture cannot establish that an event happened. If the illustrative nature could be misunderstood, identify it in the caption. These are Business Desk accuracy choices, rather than claims attributed to the three creators.

### 7. What visual style should match the post?

Choose the feeling and purpose first. A personal reflection may fit an unforced photograph. A practical lesson may fit a restrained diagram. A product tutorial needs a legible interface detail. A promotional graphic can carry business branding when the post actually concerns that business. Janis’s reaction planning and Chris’s emphasis on communication support this sequence; the specific style choices are our adaptations. [Janis on reaction](https://ozo.art/archive/want-engagement-evoke-emotional-reaction/), [Chris on layout and typography](https://thefutur.com/content/how-to-improve-your-layout-and-typography).

### 8. How much text belongs inside the image?

Use only the headline, labels, or brief explanation needed to understand the visual. Put the fuller argument and supporting detail in the caption or an accessible document. A photograph does not automatically need a text overlay. For an explanatory graphic, aim for one central idea. Janis explicitly teaches that focus. There is no established word count for every image. [One visual, one idea](https://ozo.art/archive/one-visual-one-idea).

### 9. How do we make it readable on a phone?

Business Desk’s starting design choices are one readable font family, one or two weights, clear margins, an obvious focal point, and enough empty space to separate ideas. Inspect the final picture at about 360 pixels of display width and in the platform preview. The main idea and essential labels should be readable without zooming. Enlarge type or simplify the composition when they are not.

Chris’s written typography teaching supports reading order, contrast, spacing, and purposeful layout. Its later critique sessions also credit Milka Broukhim. Our phone width and font choices are practical defaults, not their prescribed social specifications. [Typography critique](https://thefutur.com/content/learning-how-to-use-typography).

Use at least 4.5:1 text contrast as a simple design target. W3C specifies 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for large text, including images of text. Do not depend on color alone to identify a category. Add useful alt text describing the image’s meaning, and give complex charts a text explanation of the important relationship or data. [Contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [Complex image descriptions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/).

### 10. What should we avoid?

Avoid tiny paragraphs, competing headlines, unexplained shapes, unreadable screenshots, excessive effects, unrelated stock pictures, fabricated proof, and branding that overwhelms a personal story. A chart needs real data and context. A comparison needs honest labels. A conceptual curve needs an explanation that makes its illustrative purpose clear.

These are our review checks, informed by the creators’ clarity methods. Meta also specifically warns about captions unrelated to the accompanying image as part of its response to spam. That warning does not establish that every long caption is bad. [Meta’s Facebook guidance](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/cracking-down-spammy-content-facebook/).

### 11. How should the owner’s personal page differ from business pages?

Start with the owner’s actual observation and the people he wants to speak to. A relevant photograph can make a personal post feel situated in his life. A diagram can still fit when he is sharing a useful idea. Business pages can use their own visual identity, actual work, and a purpose tied to the service or product. Do not attach business branding to a personal post merely because those are the owner’s businesses.

This is our adaptation to the owner’s request. Jack’s article distinguishes personal relationships from a thematically consistent project brand, while identifying its growth explanations as hypotheses. [Personal and company brands](https://visualizevalue.com/blog/personal-brands-vs-company-brands).

### 12. How do we create a recognizable style?

For recurring graphics, choose a small palette, a type system, consistent spacing, and two or three reusable compositions. Keep enough freedom for the subject. Original personal photographs can stay natural. Recognition does not require a logo on every image. Develop the owner’s own system rather than copying a creator’s signature artwork. Jack repeatedly teaches visual consistency; our specific template count is a house choice. [Visual constraints](https://visualizevalue.com/blog/how-to-create-visual-leverage), [Consistent project identity](https://visualizevalue.com/blog/personal-brands-vs-company-brands).

### 13. What changes across Facebook, LinkedIn, and X?

Use these as production starting points, and inspect the actual composer crop before delivery. They are not claims about a universally best performing size.

| Platform | Business Desk starting canvas | Verified constraint or review step |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Personal Facebook | 1080 × 1080 for a diagram, or 1080 × 1350 when a portrait composition benefits | Preserve relevant original photo framing. Check the live crop. These sizes are house choices. |
| LinkedIn | 1080 × 1080 or 1080 × 1350 | LinkedIn recommends 1080 pixel width and an aspect ratio within 3:1 to 4:5. The first picture affects a multi image layout. |
| X | 1200 × 600 for a wide graphic, or 1080 × 1080 | X documents full display of standard single photos with aspect ratios from 2:1 through 3:4. Check previews of multiple attachments separately. |

Do not stretch photographs to fit. Recompose graphics when a new crop cuts the message. Keep essential text away from edges as a practical margin choice. Current specifications should be rechecked when a delivery depends on an upload limit. [LinkedIn image guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a525307/), [X photo guidance](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/posting-gifs-and-pictures).

### 14. When should we use one image or a sequence?

Use one image for one complete idea. Use several real pictures when each adds useful context. Use a designed sequence when the reader needs ordered steps or a developing explanation. Chris’s published carousel critique favors focused slides, coherent flow, readable contrast, and one relevant closing action. The written treatment was reviewed; the complete video was not. Apply the design method to the destination format rather than assuming old Instagram tactics describe current algorithms. [Carousel critique](https://thefutur.com/watch/instagram-carousel-design-content-marketing-advice).

X allows up to four photos in a post. LinkedIn permits up to twenty images, but an image grid is different from a document that readers page through. A LinkedIn document post can hold one PDF or another supported document, within its published limits. Choose a short useful sequence; platform maximums are not content targets. [X attachments](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/posting-gifs-and-pictures), [LinkedIn images](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a525307/), [LinkedIn documents](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a523054/document-uploads-on-linkedin-faq?lang=en).

### 15. How should the opening and caption introduce the image?

Make the opening accurate and useful. Give a photograph enough context to explain why the owner is sharing it. Name the relationship in a diagram and develop the lesson. Explain what a screenshot actually demonstrates. A carousel opening should promise what the sequence delivers. Finish with a reflection, a useful question, or a relevant action when it serves the purpose. Do not manufacture a personal story around an attractive picture. This workflow combines the existing writing guide with the inspected examples.

### 16. How will we know whether the image helped?

Choose the outcome before posting: meaningful conversation, sharing a useful idea, profile interest, a click, or a genuine inquiry. Record comments of substance, shares, saves where the platform exposes them, profile visits, clicks, and business inquiries when relevant. Compare rates per impression when comparable impression data exists, alongside the totals.

Compare similar subjects, audiences, post lengths, and time periods. Repeat the comparison across several posts rather than declaring a winner from one unusually popular example. Timing, subject, audience growth, and distribution can change the result. Ordinary post comparisons suggest what to try next; they do not isolate the image’s causal effect. This measurement plan is Codex synthesis. The research contains no controlled experiment establishing the best image frequency for the owner.

### 18. What should Business Desk do for each future post?

1. Establish the platform, audience, actual facts, and purpose. Use the writing guide to develop the post.
2. Decide whether an image has a clear job. If it does, describe that job and choose the format.
3. Choose an existing genuine asset when the subject is a real event, person, interface, or result. Otherwise prepare an original conceptual image brief when useful.
4. Match the composition, feeling, and caption to the same idea. Use only essential image text.
5. Check facts, phone readability, contrast, crop, source credits, and alt text. Explain illustrative visuals when necessary.
6. Deliver the finished copy first. When relevant, include the asset recommendation or brief and alt text. Generate an image when requested or needed to complete an authorized image deliverable. A post draft alone does not authorize publication.

## Example for the owner’s personal Facebook

For a reflection about learning to use AI, text alone is a sound starting choice. If the owner has a relevant photograph from that experience, use it with a natural crop and no required logo. Do not generate a picture that appears to document him doing something he did not do.

An optional original conceptual illustration could communicate moving from uncertainty to a manageable first step. This is a suggested direction, not a claim that an illustration will outperform the photograph or text.

**Example generation prompt:**

Create an original square conceptual illustration for a personal Facebook reflection about learning a new tool. Show a person approaching a doorway filled with tangled lines. A single clear path begins at the doorway and continues forward. Make the feeling curious and encouraging. Use a pale warm background, charcoal lines, and one muted blue accent. Keep one focal point and generous empty space. Use no text, logos, charts, statistics, or interface details. Do not depict the owner or imply this is a photograph of a real event. Make the relationship understandable at phone size.

**Suggested caption connection:** Develop the owner’s actual point about learning by trying one useful thing at a time. The wording should follow what he has really said or done.

**Suggested alt text:** A conceptual illustration shows a person facing tangled lines at a doorway, with one clear path leading forward.
