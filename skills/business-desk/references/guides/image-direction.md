# Business Desk image direction guide

Use this after the [Social Imagery Guide](imagery.md) determines that an original generated image serves the post. That guide still decides when to use an image. This guide directs how a generated image should look.

Drew Brucker’s focused [reference library](../experts/drew-brucker.md) contributes photographic direction, lighting, texture, framing, and finishing. The workflow and prompt structures below are Business Desk synthesis. They are not a reproduction of his complete course or private client prompts.

## Give the image an idea and a visual direction

Finish this sentence before prompting: “The reader should see ___ and feel or understand ___.” Choose a concrete subject, action, or relationship that carries that idea. A scene should offer something the caption cannot show by itself.

Choose one primary visual direction. These are starting choices, not a permanent style for every post.

| Direction | Useful when | Ingredients to specify |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cinematic editorial photograph | An invented scene expresses a human feeling | Subject behavior, camera distance, depth, motivated light, believable surroundings |
| Tactile photographic still life | An object or material expresses a useful metaphor | Scale, contrasting surfaces, texture, focus, deliberate studio or natural light |
| Bold editorial illustration | A conceptual relationship benefits from stylization | Original shapes, clear silhouette, limited strong palette, deliberate texture |
| Sculptural 3D concept | Form and material make an abstract contrast tangible | One central object, material response, perspective, directional light, clean separation |

For an actual person, completed client project, interface, customer result, or real event, use the genuine source asset as required by the existing imagery guide. A fictional scene can illustrate a thought, but it cannot document the owner’s life. Use business branding on a personal post only when requested.

## Replace vague adjectives with visible choices

“Beautiful, cinematic, professional, eye catching” gives little direction on its own. Specify what the viewer will actually see.

1. **Subject and behavior.** Name the subject and the meaningful action, gesture, or relationship.
2. **View and frame.** Choose a close or wide view, camera height, subject scale, and placement. Reserve empty space only when it has a purpose.
3. **Depth and focus.** Describe what sits near, in the middle, and far away. Identify what should be sharp and what can soften.
4. **Lighting.** Name the primary source, its direction, softness or hardness, and the useful contrast. Keep reflections and shadows coherent.
5. **Color.** Choose a small compatible palette and a clear accent. Strong separation can be more effective visually than saturating everything.
6. **Materials.** Specify concrete surfaces such as woven fabric, brushed metal, wet stone, rough mineral, or translucent leaves. Add relevant irregularity for a realistic scene.
7. **Constraints.** State the exact image text if needed, crop priorities, required details, and anything that would misrepresent the message.

The camera and framing choices draw on Drew’s [photography foundations](https://x.com/dcbruck/status/1686521919326081024) and [camera and subject teaching](https://x.com/dcbruck/status/1986480028352163959). His [lighting guide](https://x.com/dcbruck/status/1970543507715547498) and inspected panels make light a deliberate decision. His [imperfection guide](https://x.com/dcbruck/status/1968019789986795669) supports natural irregularity for realism. These methods do not require every image to look dark, grainy, distressed, or photographic.

## Build the prompt

Use only the fields needed for this image. This structure is our adaptation for the current image tool.

```text
Purpose: Platform, central idea, and intended feeling.
Visual direction: One compatible photographic, illustrative, or sculptural approach.
Subject and action: What matters in the scene.
Composition: Frame, camera view, subject placement, scale, depth, and focus.
Lighting: Primary source, direction, contrast, and believable shadow behavior.
Palette and materials: Specific colors and tangible surfaces.
Exact text: Required wording, or no image text.
Constraints: Facts, original assets, protected details, and relevant exclusions.
Delivery: Desired proportions, original generated file, and alt text.
```

Use references when an authorized source asset supplies an actual product, person, material, or approved visual identity. Inspect the reference first and state which properties it supplies. Develop original imagery instead of reproducing another creator’s signature composition. Reference use alone does not guarantee consistency.

Describe the desired appearance in natural language. Midjourney aspect flags, style codes, seeds, and historical interface controls are not universal instructions for other generators. Check the destination tool’s supported controls when a task depends on them. Drew’s [aspect ratio lesson](https://x.com/dcbruck/status/1948398356360839514) informs composition, not a universal platform size rule.

## Keep visual energy tied to the message

Create impact through subject scale, light, silhouette, color separation, an unexpected but understandable metaphor, or material contrast. Choose a few compatible devices. Adding every dramatic effect usually weakens the focal point.

Do not default to a pale background, a tiny icon, and a quote. Do not replace that habit with a mandatory teal and amber photograph. The subject and purpose should determine the direction. Text overlays are optional. If the message requires a diagram or precise interface detail, preserve its clarity instead of turning it into a cinematic scene.

For a recurring series, preserve an approved palette, material language, framing logic, and any typography. Vary the idea and subject. Drew’s [Pinterest case study](https://drewcbrucker.com/work/pinterest) describes piloting different forms and building reusable prompt architectures. Our exact production template and style choices are house decisions.

## Inspect and revise before delivery

Inspect the image itself, not only whether the tool returned a file. Look at the full image, important details, and a phone display preview.

* The main subject and its connection to the caption are clear at about 360 pixels of display width.
* The focal point leads attention. Background detail, bright objects, and effects do not compete excessively.
* Anatomy, object structure, perspective, shadows, reflections, and materials are plausible for the chosen style.
* Essential details survive the intended crop. Any text is exact and readable.
* The image does not imply an invented event, result, customer, or personal experience happened.
* Alt text explains what the image contributes. The original file and final prompt are saved.

Choose the largest visible weakness and make a targeted revision. Name what must stay fixed, such as the subject, pose, composition, palette, or approved product. Use the image generation tool’s editing workflow for raster revisions. Keep the original and save the revision separately. Recheck the result before choosing the final.

Drew’s [quality criteria](https://x.com/dcbruck/status/1821171411001422263) and [finishing advice](https://x.com/dcbruck/status/2077833295522382290) support review beyond generation. The checklist and editing sequence are our synthesis. His historical model comparison is not evidence about current model rankings.

## Two original personal post examples

**One useful start.** An anonymous person steps toward a glowing doorway. Wet stone, visible masonry, deep blue surroundings, and coherent warm light give the thought a concrete scene. The image illustrates taking a step without claiming that the owner was photographed there.

**Quiet progress.** A green seedling rises through rough dark stone. Close framing, translucent leaves, tactile surfaces, and a restrained shaft of sunlight support the idea of small effort accumulating.
