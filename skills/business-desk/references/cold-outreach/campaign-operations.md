# Campaign operations

Use only the sections needed. These procedures are not permission to send or change accounts.

## Diagnose missing replies

Establish the exact account, dates, segment, campaign and denominator. Inspect representative first emails, follow ups, failures and replies. Separate recent campaign results from the mailbox’s lifetime history.

| Evidence | Interpretation | Next check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| No sent records | Possible queue, account selection or scheduling problem | Confirm actual send logs |
| Hard bounce or invalid recipient | Contact quality or recipient restrictions may be involved | Read the exact response, revalidate and suppress invalid addresses |
| Mailbox full or temporary failure | Delivery may be deferred | Respect provider retry behavior; avoid a parallel sequence |
| Accepted messages, few replies | Several causes remain possible | Review targeting, offer, timing, relevance and sender health |
| Replies mostly declines | Those recipients received the message, but fit may be weak | Classify reasons and revise the segment or offer |
| Interest without meetings | Reply or handoff friction may be involved | Fulfill the promised resource and simplify next steps |
| Meetings without qualified opportunities | Qualification or positioning may be wrong | Review buyer fit and promises |
| Qualified opportunities without wins | Sales, price, scope or proof may constrain conversion | Investigate those stages before rewriting acquisition copy |

A bounce is not proof of spam folder placement. A seed inbox test only describes its test recipients. Never send deceptive control messages to unsuspecting prospects.

## Sending readiness

For launch, identify sender and recipient jurisdictions, recipient organization type, provider and volume. Resolve material unknowns before sending. US business email guidance does not automatically govern UK sole traders, EU recipients or Canadian outreach. Check current official guidance for the applicable locations and service.

Use accurate identity, a legitimate basis for contact, supported claims, a working opt out and required sender details. Suppress across campaigns for the represented sender. Do not reuse one client’s contacts or confidential evidence for another client without an appropriate basis.

For US commercial email, FTC guidance covers accurate routing and subjects, advertisement identification as applicable, a valid physical postal address, clear opt out and honoring requests within 10 business days. Business recipients are not a blanket exemption. Agency and client responsibilities can both apply. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).

Google’s personal Gmail requirements include SPF or DKIM, valid DNS, TLS and other standards. Bulk senders face additional SPF, DKIM, DMARC, alignment and applicable unsubscribe requirements. A spam rate ceiling is not an operating target or permission to contact unwilling recipients. Check current rules for the actual traffic. [Google requirements](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126).

A personal Gmail user does not control gmail.com DNS. Do not prescribe adding SPF records to that domain. Identify the sender service and inspect available headers before changes. Authentication alone does not establish inbox placement.

Do not increase volume while unexplained failures or complaints remain. Choose a batch the team can research, fulfill and handle replies for. Increase only when evidence and provider rules support it.

## Finite sequence

When no cadence is supplied, three touches over roughly two weeks can be proposed as a starting test, not an expert consensus or mandatory default. Adjust to geography, buyer rhythm, market size and prior contact.

1. First touch: relevant reason, useful offer, easy response.
2. Follow up: a specific example or resource answering a likely question.
3. Final touch: a legitimate timing or fit distinction, or a polite close. No invented urgency, customer file, prior agreement or guilt.

Stop automation and route for handling on any human reply. Suppress opt outs, explicit declines and invalid addresses. Pause affected sends on unresolved blocks. A requested future date can justify a scheduled follow up; silence alone cannot.

## Reply handling

| Reply | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Interested | Deliver the offered resource, answer the actual question and suggest a useful next step |
| Send information | Send the promised information; ask a clarifier only if necessary |
| Not now | Respect it and record a supplied date; ask about timing only when invited |
| Wrong person | Thank them; request a referral only when appropriate and verify any new contact |
| Already has a provider | Accept it; a backup offer may fit if invited and actually available |
| No, stop, unsubscribe | End the sequence and suppress, without another pitch |
| Automated or out of office | Exclude from human replies and respect the existing authorized plan |

## Measurement and experiments

Record client, campaign, segment, variant, first send date and a fixed response window. Count people for prospect rates and messages for transport rates.

* Report attempted messages, hard bounces and hard bounces divided by attempted messages.
* Call accepted messages transport acceptance, never confirmed human inbox delivery.
* Define contacted prospects as unique people with at least one accepted message in the cohort. If only attempted prospects are available, disclose that denominator instead.
* Human reply rate is unique human respondents divided by contacted prospects.
* Positive reply rate is unique prospects expressing relevant interest divided by contacted prospects. Exclude automated replies, opt outs and generic rejections.
* Report booked, held, qualified and won counts separately, with clearly named denominators. Define qualification before evaluation.

For a controlled copy test, keep audience, sender conditions, timing and response window comparable. Assign variants without cherry picking. Do not send both variants to the same prospect. Change one conceptual variable. Broader exploratory changes are possible, but do not attribute their result to one word.

Two replies versus one is insufficient evidence of a winner. At small volumes, combine directional results with reply reasons and buyer conversations. Predeclare evaluation timing and data needed for a decision; avoid daily winner switching. Report sample sizes and uncertainty.

## Daily and weekly routine

Daily: handle replies and suppressions, review failures, select qualified prospects, verify facts, prepare drafts, send only within authorization and log outcomes. Keep different services and clients in separate segments.

Weekly: examine the funnel by segment, read replies, identify the likely constraint, choose one next experiment and record what changed. Scale relevant value and response handling along with sending.
