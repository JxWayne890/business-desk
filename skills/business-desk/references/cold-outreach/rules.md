# Compatible rules and conditional tactics

These operational choices synthesize the cited works. They do not claim all seven people agree on every tactic. Source IDs resolve in [sources](sources.md).

| Rule | What to do | Evidence and boundary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| R1. Fit before volume | Define buyer role, business type, service area, use case and exclusions. Research the problem before polishing copy. | B1, B2, A3, A4. One segment does not represent an entire industry. |
| R2. One intelligible offer | Explain one useful outcome and the real service that could produce it. | A3, D2, D6, N1, N2. D6 concerns lead generation agencies; application elsewhere is our synthesis. |
| R3. Relevance before decoration | Connect a verified observation to a plausible reason for outreach. | W3, H2, H3, A1. A name token or random compliment does not explain business relevance. |
| R4. Facts before claims | Separate observation from possible implication. Be confident about capability and tentative about the unknown buyer situation. | W3, W5, D4, D5. Never manufacture loss estimates or internal processes. |
| R5. Concrete language | Prefer the reader’s words, one main point and sentences that are easy to follow. | W1, W4, J1. Clarity is the objective; punctuation and length are tools. |
| R6. Proof that belongs | Use a relevant case with accurate scope, result, time period and permission. Without it, use a real sample or explain the method honestly. | A4, A5, D1, D4, D5, N3. Recognizable names do not grant permission to imply a client relationship. |
| R7. A useful next step | Offer something concrete and make responding easy. | J1, B1, N1. A resource and a meeting solve different buyer problems. |
| R8. Respectful contact | Use accurate identity and subjects. Give the buyer room to decline. Never guilt them for silence. | J2, W6, H1. Legal and provider requirements also apply. |
| R9. Earn the follow up | Add a relevant example, resource, useful angle or timing reason. | W6, W7. Sequence length and intervals are campaign hypotheses. |
| R10. Test meaningful differences | Define what to learn. Keep conditions comparable when testing audience, offer, angle, proof or ask. | W2, N4, N5. One or two extra replies do not establish a winner. |
| R11. Diagnose the stage | Separate server acceptance, human replies, positive replies, meetings, qualified opportunities and sales. | A3, D1, N5, W7 plus operational synthesis. Silence alone does not establish spam placement or bad copy. |
| R12. Match promises to delivery | Check capacity, economics, scope and approved terms before proposing a pilot, warranty, sample or performance fee. | D3, D4, N2, N3. Risk reduction is optional, not a universal guarantee requirement. |

## Resolve disagreements

| Tension | Decision |
| :--- | :--- |
| Detailed personalization versus strong offer | The offer must be useful either way. Use segment relevance for a coherent group, adding account or person evidence when it changes the reason for contact. Narrow, valuable markets can justify deeper research. Test incremental effort and qualified outcomes. H2, H3, A1, N4. |
| Compliment first versus problem first | Alex’s January 2026 template material includes praise, while his October 6 post reports worse results from compliments. Do not force either opener. Use substantive context and frame unverified problems as possibilities. A1, A2, W5. |
| Interest question versus meeting request | A small useful ask fits a stranger. A meeting can fit expressed interest or a specific valuable working session. A vague pitch is not an audit. J1, B1, H1. |
| Concision versus fixed length | Start with the shortest draft that explains relevance, service and next step. Add detail when context requires it. Author word limits are heuristics. W1, B1. |
| Offer problem versus traffic problem | Inspect the funnel and sample first. Silence cannot isolate the cause. More volume cannot repair invalid contacts, misleading claims or a broken offer. D1, A3, N4, N5. |
| Daily volume and cadence recipes | Provider rules, sender health, client capacity, geography and outcomes determine the plan. No universal 50, 500 or 1,000 sends per day. W7 is an author heuristic, not a provider limit. |
| Open rate as delivery diagnosis | Never use a fixed open rate to declare delivery healthy or broken. Image blocking and automated loading distort opens. Use exact bounce details, authentication, provider reporting and authorized tests. H2’s open rate threshold is excluded. |
| Free work or performance pricing | Use only an approved, affordable, bounded offer. Client services and software have different delivery risks. D3 and D6 do not authorize changing a client business model. |

## Excluded advice

* Universal revenue promises, guaranteed inbox placement, fixed conversion benchmarks and unverified performance lifts.
* Treating hiring, funding or a technology lookup as proof of a problem, or AI invented buyer stories as factual research.
* Personal contact digging, fabricated senders, disguised brand relationships and methods for evading recipient protections.
* Mandatory domain rotation, warmup purchases, custom SMTP migration or tool subscriptions.
* Requiring every business to sell leads, calls or sales. The actual outcome might be a faster project handoff, fewer manual steps, a usable website or better staffing.

Write original messages from verified client facts. Never copy an expert’s example customer result into a client campaign.
