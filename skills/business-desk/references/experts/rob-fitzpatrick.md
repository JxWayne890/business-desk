## Methods

### Match the validation method to the uncertainty

Original research status: supported.

Use customer conversations to investigate real circumstances and market needs. Add prototypes or observed product use when interviews cannot resolve product or execution uncertainty. Avoid treating an interview as certain validation.

* [Customer conversations and the right validation tool](https://robfitz.com/c/writing/customer-convos-are-the-carpenter-s-hammer-pick-the-right-tool-for-the-job-validation-quads-v0-9/). Author: Rob Fitzpatrick. Locator: Opening paragraph and following discussion of market and execution risk. Interviews help uncover customer circumstances and market uncertainty. They cannot alone establish that a product can be built or that an unfamiliar experience will work.
* [Bias, validation, and context in customer development](https://robfitz.com/c/writing/bias-is-never-0-validation-is-never-100-context-and-pragmatism-in-custdev/). Author: Rob Fitzpatrick. Locator: Final paragraph, read with the three numbered practices. Reduce bias where possible, distinguish useful evidence from weak responses, and balance further learning with trying the product. Interview certainty depends on the situation.
* [Separate customer learning from product validation](https://x.com/robfitz/status/1545749544432029698). Author: Rob Fitzpatrick. Locator: Authored X post 1545749544432029698. Use conversations to understand what customers already do and why. Use a product or experiment when conversation cannot answer whether the solution works.

### Prepare a few learning goals and follow useful surprises

Original research status: candidate.

Write a few learning goals before a customer conversation, then allow follow up on unexpected evidence.

* [Prepare learning goals for customer interviews](https://x.com/robfitz/status/1610928240289275904). Author: Rob Fitzpatrick. Locator: Authored X post 1610928240289275904. Prepare a few important learning topics and follow unexpected but relevant details during the interview.
