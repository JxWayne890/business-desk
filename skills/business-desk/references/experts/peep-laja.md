## Methods

### Test message comprehension with intended buyers

Original research status: supported.

Ask intended buyers what they understand, value, and still question. Refine the message before increasing distribution. A preference test does not guarantee a sales increase.

* [How to compete and win on messaging](https://wynter.com/post/how-to-compete-and-win-on-messaging). Author: Peep Laja. Locator: You win by taking a fundamentally different position in the market section. Choose a customer relevant difference supported by the offering, then check the message with intended buyers. Different words alone do not establish different value.
* [8 lessons learned optimizing B2B SaaS pricing](https://wynter.com/post/8-lessons-learned-optimizing-b2b-saas-pricing). Author: Peep Laja. Locator: Section 5, Optimizing your pricing page will increase demand. Ask intended buyers to explain pricing and identify unanswered questions before concluding the price itself is wrong. Qualitative preference does not prove conversion improvement.
* [Check comprehension before expanding acquisition channels](https://x.com/peeplaja/status/2027394828607054202). Author: Peep Laja. Locator: Authored X post 2027394828607054202. Use feedback from intended buyers to detect confusion before distributing the same message more widely.

Original research status: candidate.

### Let research change your assumptions

Original research status: candidate.

Record what would change the message or decision before seeking buyer feedback. Treat that workflow as Codex’s application of the source.

* [Let buyer evidence change the message](https://x.com/peeplaja/status/2092595763045937252). Author: Peep Laja. Locator: Authored X post 2092595763045937252. Use research to change a weak message or assumption, rather than treating it as confirmation of an existing preference.
