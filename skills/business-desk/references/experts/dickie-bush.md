## Methods

### Make text easy to scan

Original research status: supported.

Use short paragraphs, clear points, and deliberate spacing. Keep paragraphs connected and avoid theatrical blank lines after every fragment.

* [How To Write A Great LinkedIn Post](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-write-a-great-linkedin-post). Author: Dickie Bush & Nicolas Cole. Locator: Strategy 3, Break up walls of text. Use concise sentences, visible spacing, and a clear opening followed by useful points. Joint authorship does not establish which writer originated each technique. Advice about five preview lines, guaranteed reach, and moving every link to comments is dated and is excluded from current defaults.
* [Formatting Techniques For A Newsletter: 5 Simple Tips](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/formatting-techniques-for-a-newsletter-5-simple-tips). Author: Dickie Bush & Nicolas Cole. Locator: Tips 1, 3, and 4. Make the topic apparent when skimmed. Outline before drafting and present lists as short points. Application to social posts is an adaptation from newsletter teaching, with a second LinkedIn source supporting readability. Joint authorship is explicit.

### State who the post helps and why it matters

Original research status: supported.

Choose a truthful opening with a specific audience, topic, and useful outcome. Do not hide necessary context behind a curiosity tease.

### Review a draft with fresh perspective

Original research status: supported.

Separate drafting and editing when practical. Read aloud or review on another screen. The exact delay is flexible.

* [Editing checks](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1999147978334843001). Author: Dickie Bush. Locator: Post 1999147978334843001, author text. The advice combines conversational wording, a particular reader, separate editing, reading aloud, and review on another screen. Promotional claims about money and improvement magnitude are excluded.
* [Draft then edit](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1595229693007073283). Author: Dickie Bush. Locator: Post 1595229693007073283, author text. He separates drafting from later reading aloud and editing. A pause can improve perspective, but urgent business posts do not require a full day delay.

### Choose an opening suited to the message

Original research status: candidate.

A real moment, direct observation, or useful question can start a post. The examples are choices, not universal performance rules.

* [Opening options](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1725182885731213686). Author: Dickie Bush. Locator: Post 1725182885731213686, author text. The post offers several kinds of first sentence, including a real moment, a vulnerable statement, and a direct assertion. Use a true event or useful question. Provocation is optional and promises of instant attention are not validated.

### Explain a verified before and after

Original research status: candidate.

Describe the initial problem, work performed, result, limitation, and relevant next step. Never invent customer outcomes.

* [Case study structure](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1980756844273353152). Author: Dickie Bush. Locator: Post 1980756844273353152, author text. The post supplies a before, intervention, outcome, qualification, and next step sequence. Apply only to verified customer facts and appropriately permitted details. Avoid universal claims that results are simple, cheap, or assured.

### Remove words that add no meaning

Original research status: candidate.

Trim redundant fillers while keeping a conversational voice.

* [Remove filler](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1723877470531641628). Author: Dickie Bush. Locator: Post 1723877470531641628, author text. He suggests deleting unnecessary filler during editing. Preserve words that change meaning or make the owner’s voice natural.
